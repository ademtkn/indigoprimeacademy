Cambridge & Singapore

Cambridge sets the international standard of what pupils learn. The Singapore method decides how they learn it: actively, together and with real understanding.

Cambridge curriculum

An international standard for every subject

Cambridge International Education is part of the University of Cambridge. Its programmes and exams are used by schools in more than 160 countries and accepted by universities around the world.

Every subject has a clear curriculum framework for each year. Teachers plan lessons against these goals, and pupils take externally marked exams at the end of Lower Secondary, IGCSE and A Level.

The programme values thinking skills as much as knowledge: analysing, explaining, experimenting and writing clearly.

The Cambridge learner is…

  • Confident in working with information and ideas
  • Responsible for themselves and others
  • Reflective, able to develop their ability to learn
  • Innovative and ready for new challenges
  • Engaged intellectually and socially

Singapore method

Mathematics you can see and understand

Singapore is among the world leaders in international studies of mathematics. Its method moves every new idea through three steps, so that pupils truly understand before they practise.

1 · Concrete

Hands on

Pupils first explore an idea with real objects: cubes, counters, measuring tools, paper models.

2 · Pictorial

Draw it

Then they draw the problem. The famous bar model turns a word problem into a clear picture.

3 · Abstract

Write it

Only then do they move to numbers and symbols, now with a solid understanding behind them.

In every lesson

Learning structures that involve every pupil

Beyond mathematics, our teachers use Singapore collaborative learning structures in all subjects. Nobody sits quietly at the back: everyone thinks, speaks and helps.

Pair work

Pupils explain ideas to a partner, check each other and take turns answering.

Teams of four

Small mixed teams solve problems together, each pupil with a clear role.

Thinking time

A pause before answering so that every pupil, not only the fastest, has time to think.

Every voice counts

Structured turn-taking means every pupil takes part, every lesson.

Together

What a lesson looks like

  1. 1

    A real question

    The lesson begins with a problem or question linked to life.

  2. 2

    Explore

    Pupils try ideas with objects, drawings or experiments.

  3. 3

    Discuss

    Pairs and teams compare methods and explain their thinking.

  4. 4

    Practise

    Focused practice, from simple to challenging tasks.

  5. 5

    Reflect

    Pupils check what they have learned and what comes next.
